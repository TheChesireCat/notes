## Sources

- A.-L. Barabási, *Network Science*, Cambridge University Press (2016); free online at [networksciencebook.com](https://networksciencebook.com). It has the intuition, history and empirical evidence.
- M. E. J. Newman, *Networks*, 2nd ed., Oxford University Press (2018). It has the mathematics: matrix methods, generating functions, algorithms and measurement.

## How this course is organised

**Topic pages** take one concept at a time and combine both books, Barabási's picture alongside Newman's derivations. Examples are random graphs, the configuration model, preferential attachment, percolation and community structure. They are being written; see [#4](https://github.com/TheChesireCat/notes/issues/4) for the list and progress.

Until each topic page exists, the **chapter notes** below are the reference:
- **Barabási**: notes for chapters 0–10, plus shorter summaries of chapters 1, 2 and 7.
- **Newman**: notes for chapters 1–5 (empirical networks), 6, 7, 8 and 10.

Each set of chapter notes will be folded into the matching topic pages.
